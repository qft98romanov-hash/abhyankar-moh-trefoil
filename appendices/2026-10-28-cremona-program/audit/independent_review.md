## Mathematical and implementation checks

1. **Base formulas.** The coefficient field relation, the polynomials
   `P`, `F`, `H`, the companion `W`, and the two inverse components agree
   between the code and the manuscript. The inverse congruences are tested
   in both directions modulo `x^5`. The cancellation certificate for the
   companion uses the already checked parameter-component identity and the
   nonzero factor `b*x^5` in an integral domain. It is not an argument from
   the Jacobian determinant alone.
2. **Transfers.** The modular numerator tests use the actual base maps,
   `p_N`, and the constructed correction. Reduction modulo the full `x^N`
   is legitimate because these substitutions fix `x`. The inverse
   numerator is reduced using precisely the companion correction from the
   forward construction. The factors and their inverse factors are the
   ones in the manuscript, so polynomiality in both directions and
   injectivity of localization establish both global compositions.
3. **Finite computational range.** The code tests `N=1,2,3` in the recorded
   run, materializing both relative maps for `N=1` and using modular and
   factored certificates in general. The manuscript's all-`N` argument is
   an induction and a uniform transfer proof. Finite tests are not used to
   infer the universal result. Degree equalities in the JSON are likewise
   finite checks.
4. **Wildness.** The base coefficient fails the length-one divisibility
   criterion. For the family, the normalized inner coordinate has a
   quadratic coefficient congruent to `-x` modulo `x^2`, which cannot be
   divisible by `x^(N+1)`. The reduced-quadruple argument uses the tame
   factorization statement and uniqueness from Edo's classification;
   it is not inferred from finite computations. The hypotheses needed to
   apply the Umirbaev--Yu theorem are restricted to characteristic zero.
5. **Cremona subgroup.** The balanced monomial maps pass the column-parity
   criterion. Laurent shears are conjugates of polynomial shears by the
   specified even scaling. The transfer scaling `D_N` normalizes the
   Laurent subgroup; its own membership in the standard Cremona subgroup
   is not incorrectly assumed for odd `N`. The displayed base and transfer
   factorization orders are consistent with right-to-left geometric
   composition.
6. **Stable tameness.** The use of the published theorem is for the
   subgroup generated by maps fixing a coordinate. Relative maps and the
   elementary factors of the external tame maps belong to that subgroup.
   The conclusion gives a sufficient stabilization by three variables;
   neither a minimal stabilization nor an explicit six-variable tame word
   is claimed.
7. **Arithmetic and the earlier candidates.** Direct differentiation gives
   `D(V)=0` and `D(s)=1` in the displayed five-variable construction. Both
   new coordinate changes are polynomial by exact divisibility at `t=0`.
   Consequently the kernel, the slice hypersurface, and the extended Rees
   ring have the stated polynomial descriptions. These are specific
   consequences of the supplied rectifier, not solutions of general
   cancellation or linearization problems.
8. **Scope safeguards.** The distinct `+4t` parametrization is not claimed
   to be rectified. Wildness is a property of the selected maps; it does
   not exclude another tame rectifier of the same curve. No classification
   of the whole Cremona group or proof of the general spatial embedding
   conjecture is asserted.

## Findings raised during review

- The status table originally said that a "complete descent criterion"
  was unavailable, while the program section already supplies an exact
  cocycle criterion. The table was corrected to distinguish this formal
  criterion from an effective classification of the fields of
  rectification.
- The original false-identity and mutated-inverse negative controls
  computed failures with a separate explicit `raise`. The reviewer
  requested that they call the actual `Certificate.zero` checker, so that
  the controls test the working guard rather than a duplicated condition.
  The nondivisible-numerator control already calls the actual division
  routine. Both identity controls were subsequently changed to use a fresh
  `Certificate().zero` recorder, and the revised implementation was reread.

No substantive algebraic defect was found in the checked formulas,
factorization arguments, or stated boundaries. This verdict concerns the
scope above and remains part of the research branch under verification.
