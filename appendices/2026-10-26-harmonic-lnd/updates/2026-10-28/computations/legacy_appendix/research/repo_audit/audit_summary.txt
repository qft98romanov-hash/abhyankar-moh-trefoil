GENERAL TRANSFER ARGUMENT
For B=k[x]/(m), a one-variable polynomial c(y) with unit linear coefficient
and nilpotent higher coefficients is an automorphism of B[y]. Finite iteration
in the ideal J generated by higher coefficients stabilizes because J is nilpotent.
Both inverse identities follow: the second can be proved by cancelling the unit
factor in c(v)-c(y)=(v-y)(c1+n). The quotient correction phi is then defined in
the full ring B, not just its reduced quotient. This proves exact divisibility
of both full numerators by m, retaining repeated factors. Applying A^{-1} to
the two centre identities proves the inverse numerator divisible by m. Both
maps are inverse after localizing at m; since both maps are polynomial and the
original polynomial ring injects into its localization, both identities hold
globally, including every exceptional fibre. The Jacobian is supplementary,
not the invertibility argument. The constant-m case is handled separately.

BASE SIMPLIFICATION AND LND
See proof.tex for a ready-to-include Russian proof fragment. With b=1-a,
v=z+a*x*y, A=-1+a*x^2-a*x^4,
q=-2*x^5+(6-2*a)*x^3-2*b*x and h0=b*x*v^2+A*v+q,
the base coordinate is H=h0(x,v)-b*x^5*y. The polynomial f in the repository
is the inverse of h0 modulo x^5. Thus W=a*(v-f(x,H))/x^5 is polynomial.
The inverse follows immediately from v=f(x,T)+b*x^5*V,
y=(h0(x,v)-T)/(b*x^5), z=v-a*x*y, where the second division is exact.
The associated Jacobian derivation D=H_z*d_y-H_y*d_z equals
b*x^5*d_v+(2*b*x*v+A)*d_y. Therefore D is locally nilpotent, D(W)=1,
and ker(D)=K[x,H]. This is an explicit application of a classical finite
inverse construction, not a claim of a new general lemma.
For harmonic hH=H(x,x*z-y^2+2,y), DH=hH_z*d_y-hH_y*d_z has slice -qH,
kernel K[x,hH], and exact nilpotency orders 3 on y and 5 on z.
